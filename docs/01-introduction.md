# Chapter 1 — Product Vision & Strategic Framing

## Why This Project Exists

I wanted to build a SaaS product that would serve three purposes:

1. Refresh and deepen my web development skills
2. Explore React and Next.js in a real-world scenario
3. Design a monetizable product rather than a purely technical demo

Instead of building another abstract CRUD application, I chose a concept with real-world traction: a live webcam directory platform.

The idea is simple:

Display categorized live streams of Greek locations (initially), monetize traffic through advertising and premium features, and architect the system for global expansion.

This project is intentionally framed as a startup engineering case study rather than just a coding exercise.

---

## Why Live Webcams?

Live webcam platforms have several characteristics that make them attractive from a product perspective:

- Evergreen demand (weather, tourism, city views)
- High engagement time
- Repeat visits
- Strong SEO-driven traffic
- Multiple monetization layers

Established platforms demonstrate that this niche generates significant traffic and advertising revenue.

The opportunity is not in cloning an existing product, but in:

- Focusing geographically (Greece-first)
- Optimizing for mobile
- Structuring the platform with clean modern architecture
- Building scalable foundations from day one

---

## Greece-First Strategy

Instead of launching globally, I intentionally narrow the scope to Greece.

Reasons:

- Lower SEO competition
- Strong tourism traffic
- Clear market identity
- Manageable dataset
- Faster validation cycle

However, the database schema and routing system are designed to support multi-country expansion without structural changes.

This reflects a common startup principle:

> Validate locally. Architect globally.

---

## Product Scope (MVP)

The initial version focuses on:

- Displaying embedded YouTube live streams
- Categorizing by city and type (Beach, City, Port, etc.)
- SEO-friendly dynamic routing
- Server-side rendering for discoverability
- Advertising-based monetization

The MVP intentionally avoids:

- Hosting proprietary camera streams
- Building a distributed streaming infrastructure
- Scraping copyrighted content
- Overengineering early features

The goal is disciplined execution, not premature complexity.

---

## Engineering Goals

This project is not just about building a website.

It is designed to demonstrate:

- System architecture thinking
- Folder structure discipline
- Scalable schema planning
- SEO-aware development
- Monetization-aware design
- Clean separation of concerns

Each chapter of this case study reflects a deliberate engineering decision.
